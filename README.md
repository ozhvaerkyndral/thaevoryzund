# Thaevoryzund

> An unread page keeps the shadow of an eclipse beneath a dragon's eyelid.

## The Bookshop After Closing

After closing, the bookseller turns every book so that its spine faces the wall. He says titles become restless in darkness. Left unwatched, they exchange their words, and customers return with stories that no longer belong to the covers they purchased.

One book cannot be turned. It stands open on a stone lectern at the back of the shop. Its pages are pale grey, its binding black, and its first line is always hidden beneath the shadow of a bird that never appears overhead.

The bookseller has tried moving the lamp. He has tried opening the shutters and carrying a candle around the lectern. The shadow remains, narrow and perfectly still, covering the same seven words.

He dusts around it with a feather made of thin copper. Whenever the feather touches the paper, a bell rings somewhere outside the town.

## A Reader Carrying a Blank Letter

A reader came late one evening with a blank letter folded in her pocket. She said she had received it on the day of an eclipse. Although no writing appeared on the paper, she had recognized the handwriting immediately.

The bookseller asked whose handwriting it was. She said that answering would require a name she had not used since childhood.

He placed the letter beside the open book. The bird's shadow lengthened toward it. A faint line appeared along the fold, resembling a path crossing a field at dusk.

The reader remembered the field. She had walked there with a person who carried a little brass bell. Whenever the bell rang, she was told to close her eyes until the birds stopped moving. She had never known what she was being protected from seeing.

## The Margin Beyond the Window

The book's outer margin widened. In the empty paper appeared a window, and beyond the window lay the remembered field. The sky above it held a dark circle surrounded by pale fire.

Someone stood beneath the eclipse. Their face was hidden by distance, but the brass bell could be seen clearly in one hand. The other held a book with its spine turned toward the reader.

The bookseller lifted his copper feather. The distant figure lifted a matching feather. For a moment, every book in the shop leaned outward from its shelf, as though listening through its closed pages.

The reader reached toward the margin. Her fingertips passed through the paper and returned smelling of dry grass. On one finger clung a small black seed, shaped like the shadow of a flying bird.

## What Closed Its Eye

The eclipse was the pupil of a creature sleeping beyond the field. Its eyelid curved along the horizon. Its scales formed the low hills, and its folded wing cast the darkness beneath which the distant figure stood.

The bookseller had seen it before, in the spaces between printed letters. He had once spent an entire winter copying a sentence from the open book. When he finished, the sentence had become a drawing of that same closed eye, and he had found a brass bell beneath his desk.

He still kept the bell in a drawer. It had no clapper. Now, as the black seed rested on the lectern, the bell began to sound without moving.

In the margin, the figure beneath the eclipse opened their book. A small child stood inside its pages, eyes tightly closed, waiting for the birds to settle.

## Seven Words in Borrowed Light

The reader placed her blank letter over the bird's shadow. Light passed through the paper, revealing marks along the book's first line:

*Thae — vory — zund — aelith.*

She understood the marks as a name, though she could not say whose. The copper feather warmed in the bookseller's hand. The seed split, releasing a thread of light that entered the letter's fold.

The seven hidden words became visible for a moment. Neither reader nor bookseller could remember them afterward. They remembered instead a sound: somebody taking a slow breath before telling a child that it was safe to look.

The child in the distant book opened her eyes. The eclipse narrowed. Beyond the field, the creature lowered its enormous lid, and the pale fire along its edge became the first line of morning.

## The Page Left Unturned

The reader unfolded her letter. It was no longer blank. A field had been drawn across it in delicate lines, and at its edge stood two figures holding a bell between them. The path led toward a house she had believed forgotten.

She thanked the bookseller using the childhood name she had brought with her. The name belonged to neither of them, but the brass bell grew quiet when it was spoken.

After she left, he turned the shop's books toward the wall. The volume on the lectern remained open. Its first line was visible now, but another bird's shadow rested farther down the page.

He put the copper feather beside it and extinguished the lamp.

Outside, well beyond the town, a bell rang once. Somewhere beneath a closed eyelid, the unread page held its light until the next reader was ready.
